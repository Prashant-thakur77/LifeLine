# Backtest results

Generated by `pnpm backtest` from the candles saved in `candles/`. Don't edit this file by hand: rerun the command.

**Latency is assumed, not measured yet.** Partly measured. The keeper's decision and the vault's NEAR transaction are measured in research/latency/RESULTS.md, but 1Click's delivery to Hyperliquid isn't, until a rescue moves real money. So p50 is 1Click's own 30 s estimate plus one 5 s keeper poll, and p95 is a deliberately pessimistic 120 s.

## The result

On 18,000 random BTC, ETH and SOL positions, using real Hyperliquid 4h candles from 2024-07-26 to 2026-11-06, each held for 7 days:

- **Without protection, 34.2% were liquidated. With Lifeline, 20.6% at 35 s latency and 20.6% at 120 s.**
- **If every candle's adverse move takes one minute, a stress test harsher than most real markets, those become 34.2%, 29.1% and 29.4%.** The real figure sits between the two runs.
- **On average, Lifeline costs money, because it buys survival, not profit: -$0.15 per position without it, -$1.14 with it.** When it saved a position, that position finished $68.74 better on average. When the position was liquidated anyway, it finished -$49.98 on average against no protection, because the reserve went with it. 4,088 of 18,000 positions lost more than the fee this way.

## Flash-crash stress test

Candles hide speed, so this run makes the opposite assumption: every candle's adverse move happens in its first minute. That's harsher than most real markets, so read it as a bound on how much speed can hurt, not as a forecast. At that pace the fall from trigger to liquidation usually takes only seconds, so a rescue rarely gets there first, and the gap between the two latencies matters less than the crash itself.

## Honest failure cases

Real positions from these runs where Lifeline didn't help.

**The reserve ran out in a falling market.** SOL 10× long, opened 2025-10-05 12:00 UTC at $232.7 with $100.00 margin and $50.00 reserve. The candle that ended it opened 2025-10-10 20:00 UTC and reached a low of $137.51. Lifeline fired 5 rescues at 35 s latency, spent $50.00 of the reserve, and the position was still liquidated. Result: -$128.33 with Lifeline, -$76.93 without.

**A cap or the cooldown held the next rescue back.** No position in this run failed this way.

**The crash beat the rescue (flash stress test).** ETH 20× long, opened 2025-09-25 08:00 UTC at $4,006.7 with $100.00 margin and $50.00 reserve. The candle that ended it opened 2025-09-25 16:00 UTC and reached a low of $3,821.7. Lifeline fired 1 rescue at 35 s latency, spent $25.00 of the reserve, and the position was still liquidated. Result: -$63.01 with Lifeline, -$62.69 without.

## Method

- **Positions:** 1,000 per coin, leverage (5×, 10×, 20×) and side, so 18,000 per run, with seeded random entry times. Each scenario runs the same positions.
- **Health:** equity divided by maintenance margin, on a single cross position. Maintenance is half the initial margin at max leverage, per Hyperliquid's docs: 1.25% for BTC, 2% for ETH and 2.5% for SOL.
- **Rescue:** the keeper's own rules. It looks every 5 s, fires below health 1.5, sends `(needed + fee) × 1.01` to reach health 2, cut to the caps and reserve, waits 120 s between rescues, and never sends under $2.00. Money arrives after the latency, less the $0.32 fee.
- **Inside a candle:** the adverse extreme comes first, which is the harshest order. The price then moves at an even pace, or in the stress test takes one minute for the adverse move.
- **Liquidation:** at health 1. Hyperliquid's docs say remaining collateral stays with the trader when the book can close the position, so that's assumed. A backstop liquidation would lose it, so real losses could be larger.

## What this doesn't show

- It ignores funding, trading fees, price impact and the 1% slippage 1Click allows. All of them make real results somewhat worse.
- Each account holds one position. A real account with several can behave differently.
- Random entries have no edge, so their average result is near zero by nature. A trader with real edge would keep more of what a saved position later earns, and this test can't measure that.
- It says nothing about whether a trader should hold a losing position at all. A top-up keeps a position alive; it doesn't make it a good trade.
- Hyperliquid launched in 2023, and its daily candles go back to 2020-08-19, so the older ones must come from elsewhere. They're used only in the sensitivity table.
- Positions and entry times are random, not real traders' behaviour.
