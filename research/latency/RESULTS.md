The keeper decision is everything the keeper does between reading health below the trigger and sending the rescue: the same calls in the same order, against the real Hyperliquid and 1Click APIs. The quotes are dry here, so nothing is deposited. The policy check itself takes well under a millisecond and isn't listed.

All 20 attempts finished.

The NEAR transaction is the vault's `rescue`, signed with the keeper's function-call key and waited on until it executed: the storage registration, the USDC transfer and the callback. It ran on testnet, where blocks come at the same pace as mainnet.

After that, 1Click has to swap and deliver to Hyperliquid. That part is not measured until a rescue moves real money. 1Click's own estimate in every quote was 30 s.

Adding it up: the keeper looks every 5 s, so it sees a drop 2.5 s later on average and at most 5.0 s. With 1Click's estimate, a rescue would take about 38.8 s at p50 and 42.1 s at p95, from the moment health crosses the trigger to USDC in the Hyperliquid account. Real rescues replace this estimate in `/api/stats`, which reports the measured time from detection to credit.
