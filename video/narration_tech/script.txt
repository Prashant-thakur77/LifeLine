This is a technical walkthrough of Lifeline: how a rescue works, why a hacked keeper can't steal, and how to check every claim yourself.
Health is your cross-margin account value, divided by the maintenance margin Hyperliquid requires. As it approaches one, Hyperliquid liquidates.
The keeper reads it from Hyperliquid every five seconds. Below your trigger, one point five by default, a rescue starts.
It sizes the top-up to bring health back to your target, adds 1Click's flat fee, and caps it at your limits and your reserve.
Then it asks 1Click for a quote, from USDC on NEAR into your Hyperliquid perps balance, and checks 1Click's signature before trusting a single field.
A pure policy engine checks sixteen rules, including the recipient, the refund account, both caps, the cooldown, the quote's age and slippage. Anything it can't read blocks the rescue.
Then the vault on NEAR checks again, on chain. It verifies 1Click's signature itself, and reads the deposit address, recipient, amount and refund account from the signed quote.
The keeper's only key is a NEAR function-call key. It can call rescue on the vault, and nothing else: no withdrawals, no transfers, no policy changes.
So we let anyone attack it. The attack lab holds a keeper's real key and sends rescue tricks to the testnet vault. Every one is refused on chain, and your browser reads the refusal back from a public node.
Paying itself. Paying the attacker. An edited quote. Over the cap. A stale quote. Keeping the refund. A reused deposit address. Seven tricks, seven refusals.
The verify page checks the rest in your browser: the keeper's keys, and the vault's code against the chain.
The vault is a reproducible build. Rebuild the commit it names, and you get the exact code hash deployed on testnet.
Explanations come from a model on NEAR AI Cloud, in attested hardware. Each answer is signed inside the enclave. Your browser checks that signature, finds the key in a fresh hardware report, and verifies Intel's signature on it.
The money path runs end to end on testnet: a signed quote, a real NEAR deposit, and the USDC in a Hyperliquid perps balance fourteen point six seconds later.
Under the hood: Hyperliquid for health, the policy engine, a Rust vault on NEAR, NEAR Intents for the money, and NEAR AI for the words.
